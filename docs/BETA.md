# Shift beta 16: finding out is the game, everywhere

Published 2026-11-06 as a private page, the same link as every beta:
**https://claude.ai/artifact/4c5QqYTmZdn5iKcutCPZJx**
(only the author can open it until he shares it from the page's Share menu).
Rebuild with `npm run build` and republish `dist/shift.embed.html` to the same
link. The build id is baked in, so an installed copy picks the new one up.

**What changed in beta 16 (2026-11-06):** the find-out row is on every
problem ticket now, not just *The late one*. Ask the person, ask the people
around it, or look at the evidence; each tells you something different,
everyone near a problem has a view, and the evidence rules reasons out
without naming one. Acting on the wrong reason costs that person's trust and
makes it worse. A fix that fails twice rules its reasons out on the ticket.
*Round two* has a crew to ask, who mostly think the answer is more checking.
Every module but *Rush week* now passes the test that a player who just
believes the first answer loses clearly (`KILLGATE-ask.md`).

**What changed in beta 15 (2026-11-06):** from your note that it felt like
finding the right answer while keeping under forty hours. *The late one*'s
ticket now has two rows. **Find out**: ask Brent, ask around the crew, or
pull his timecards. What he tells you depends on how much he trusts you (the
ticket says), Kev and Tyler know some of it and Tyler has a view, and the
timecards rule a reason out without naming one. Nobody's answer comes
labelled true. **Do**: act on what agrees, or say the standard at the huddle
while you find out. Every module's end screen now says what the cause was,
and replays your exact month five other ways so you can see what each
instinct would have got. Wrong moves say why they were wrong. The other
modules get the same treatment in beta 16 (`REVIEW-loop.md`,
`KILLGATE-ask.md`).

**What changed in beta 14 (2026-11-05):** *Pulled off the floor* rebuilt.
Why Kev is not ready to run the floor is now a hidden cause: never shown the
pieces, never seen as in charge by the crew, won't say what he doesn't know,
or ready and the problem is you. Sit down with him and find out, or guess;
whatever you did not find lands on him in the audit weeks. The four
hand-over cards are gone; the ticket replaces them. Gate: doing nothing fell
from 40% to 13% (`FRAME-pulled.md`).

**What changed in beta 13 (2026-11-05):** group sessions run on one screen.
On the coach's *Group sessions* tab, *Run it on this screen* starts the
session's module with a facilitator's strip above the week: what to say
before the brief, the line for this week where there is one, the standing
rule folded. The room watches the ticket, the facilitator takes hands and
taps what the room picks, Friday is read aloud. The end screen carries the
close, the debrief questions, the reveal (folded until the room has
answered) and the commitment card. *Notes to print* is the facilitator's
page; on a laptop the page widens for a projector.

**What changed in beta 12 (2026-11-05):** the coach's side. The title
screen asks whether you are a trainee or a coach. A coach keeps a roster:
each trainee's seat and department, the modules assigned in the order they
should be played, opening one at a time or all at once. Each trainee gets an
assignment code (about 200 characters) to paste on the title screen; their
track then shows only what was assigned, under the coach's name. They hand
back a progress code (plays, reads, commitment, sign-off; never the save or
the debrief answers), or send it from the page when signed in on claude.ai;
the coach pastes it, or fetches the sent ones, and the roster shows each
module's state. A third tab holds six group sessions to run with a room,
each built on a module that exists, with the plan, the questions, the reveal
and the commitment card (`GROUPS.md`). `FRAME-coach.md` has the breadth
audit the author asked for and its verdict.

## How to test beta 8

Put your plant's system name on the profile and play *What the system sees*.
The question: does the chain from a Friday clock-in to Corey's promise read
true, and what would you add from how your plant actually runs it (that is
the coach's part of the debrief, on purpose)? Then *Say it once*: is the
tally the thing you'd do, and does the bar dropping feel earned?

## How to test beta 7

Play *The other side* as the welding supervisor. One question: when the
first ticket came back with your crew's name on it, what did you do, and
would you have done it a week earlier on your real floor?

## How to test beta 6

Make a profile as the operations manager and play *The late one* or *Where
it waits*. Two questions: when you asked Kev rather than told him, did it feel
like a real difference or a label; and did going down to fix it yourself
tempt you, and what did the report say about it after?

## How to test beta 5

Make a second profile as one of your leads (Start over from the title, or
Change on the track) and play *The late one* or *The new hand* from the lead
hand's seat. Two questions: does taking it to Kev feel like what a lead does,
or like a button; and is the table-time cost of leading about right for a
lead hand who also works a station?

## How to test beta 4

Play one of the three new modules, whichever your profile puts first. The
question for each: does the thing it is about feel like the thing it is
about? In *Where it waits*, did you look before you spent the hours? In
*Monday, 7:00*, did you ask Brent? In *The new hand*, did you sit with Sam?
And the one that matters most: which of the three would your leads believe?

## How to test beta 3

1. Open the link. **Start over** if you have a beta 2 save, or keep it: an
   old save asks for a profile before the track.
2. Fill in the profile as yourself, then as one of your leads. Look at the
   order the track gives each of you and the reason under each module. Is
   the order one you would assign?
3. Play one module and read the end report. The question is whether the
   read (Solid, Getting there, Not yet) and the made-and-missed lists would
   start the debrief conversation, and whether anything in "also on the
   floor, unrated" should have been in the module's focus.
4. Then decide the order of the next build (`FRAME-departments.md`, last
   section): the three new modules first, or the three seats first.

## What is in it

- The Supervisor track: eight modules, 6 to 8 simulated weeks each, about 15
  to 25 minutes a module.
- The week screen: the pixel bay (tap a person to see how they look), the
  hours bar against a forty-hour week, and the inbox as work tickets: the
  module's problem first, requests, your standing week, an hour with someone,
  welding a booth yourself, walking the floor. Every option shows its hours.
- The Friday report, the crew, the log, the end report (what you met, what
  came from what, what is still open, the practice profile), the debrief with
  coach sign-off, and save codes.
- The beta switch on the track screen opens every module at once. Without it,
  each module opens when the one before it is debriefed and signed off.

## How to test it (from beta 1; still valid for voice, look and length)

1. Open the link on your phone. Start the Supervisor track. Play **The week**
   honestly, the way you would run your own week.
2. Read the end report. Then play it again: a replay draws new hidden causes,
   so the same choices do not give the same week.
3. Do the debrief as if you were the trainee, and sign it off as the coach.
4. Turn on the beta switch and play **The late one** and **Rush week**.
5. Write down, as you go, anything from this list:

| Look for | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| A line that does not sound like a person | The voice is the thing a trainee will believe or not |
| A scenario that would never happen on a real floor | The pilot's first question: which scenarios feel fake |
| An option you wanted that is not there | Missing levers make it a quiz |
| A result that felt unfair, not just bad | The difference between a lesson and a dice roll |
| A screen where you did not know what to do | Rule 6: every state says why and what changes it |
| How long a module took | The plan guesses 15 to 25 minutes |
| Whether the debrief questions would start a real conversation | The debrief is the product |

Notes can go anywhere. Comments on the artifact page sent to Claude reach the
session that published it while that session is alive; a new session should
read this file and `CONCEPT.md` first.

## Known gaps, stated plainly

- **Two modules separate the styles weakly.** In *The week*, a work order
  fixes most extractor causes, so most styles get through; the lesson lives in
  the hours and the refusals more than in the win. In *Pulled off the floor*,
  doing nothing still gets through 45% of the time. Both pass the gate; both
  are first candidates for tuning after your verdict.
- **Every number is a first guess** carried from the kill gate: capacity,
  hours, odds, lags. The gate shows the loop has the right shape, not the
  right calibration.
- **Text repeats on replays.** About 460 lines, two or three variants for the
  frequent ones. The voice has not had your verdict yet.
- **The focus areas are the agent's first cut** (`src/data/focus.data.js`).
  Cut, add or rename; a module's `focus` line is all that changes.
- **One bay.** The profile takes any department and seat; every module's
  floor, crew and text are the welding supervisor's. `FRAME-departments.md`
  costs the rest.
- **Saves live on one device.** The artifact page and an installed copy keep
  separate saves. A save code moves progress anywhere.
- **Coach sign-off is honour system.** Anyone holding the phone can sign.
  Fine for a pilot with willing people; not for anything official.
- **Only the Supervisor track.** Lead, Manager, CI/Lean, Facilitator and the
  leadership group are planned (`PLAN.md`), not built.
- **No Pages site** while the repository is private; the beta ships as one
  file.
